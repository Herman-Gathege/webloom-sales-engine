# Anne — Sprint 2

> **Where we are:** the app runs, and you can already click through the Lead List and a Lead
> Detail with its history — the eight leads are invented. **Your piece this sprint:** the
> seller's eye. You are the only one of us who can catch a screen that makes no sense to the
> person who will actually use it.

## Your mission

Give the Lead Desk a seller's opinion, and leave it in writing this time.

## What to do

- Write down the three things you started in Sprint 1, short and in your own words: the seller's
  journey in three or four steps, what the **Lead List** must show, and what the **Lead Detail**
  must show.
- Add 3–5 concrete recommendations — what is confusing, what is missing, what is noise.
- Add one realistic "good lead" to the sample data, so the demo opens on something a seller
  would recognise. Made-up business name and phone number only, never a real contact.
- If Sharon's importer needs the wording a rejected row shows, write it — plain language, one
  line, telling a person what to fix.
- Review the two PRs when they open: Sharon's importer and Herman's filters. One question each
  is enough — does the rejected-row message make sense to a person, and can you find the lead
  you want in three clicks?
- Do not spend your evening on Docker. If the app will not start, ask in the group.

This is review work because you are the only person who can do it. Nobody else on the team can
tell whether a screen makes sense to a seller, and the sprint is only worth anything if the
list it produces is one you would actually work.

## Done means

- Your notes are in a PR, not in your head.
- Your example lead shows up in the app.
- Both PRs have your comments, or an explicit "nothing to add".

## Bring back

- A short PR with your notes and your example lead.
- Your review comments on the two PRs.
- One thing you would change before we show this to a real business.
